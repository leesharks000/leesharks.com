# Lee Sharks and Training-Layer Literature: selection review

1 November 2026 · Research and editorial recommendations · No repository changes made

**The concern is justified. The present Lee Sharks census systematically underselects the creative practices and authorship research closest to its subject. Claude's replacement list improves the balance, but does not yet adequately map that field. The TLL selection has stronger infrastructure coverage, while underrepresenting machine reception becoming composition.**

This review reads the current census definitions and records in the two GitHub repositories, the current TLL home page, the authorship census's anchoring texts, and primary sources for proposed additions. It is a selection review, not a verification of every factual field in the 34 existing records. Proposed additions below are researched candidates, not completed source-ledger records. Publisher and artist descriptions are identified as such; their claims are not independently established merely by being quoted.

## The selection error precedes the individual picks

The Lee Sharks organizing question is: “Whose authorship does it keep, under what name, and how does it hold a name the author chose?” That is a legitimate question. But the census defines its AI area as “law, policy, certification and litigation on who or what can be an author.” The area definition largely excludes the making of AI-mediated literary authorship before the search begins. Its six AI-area records are legal, policy, certification or legal-commentary resources, with no comparable strand of documented creative practice.

It also narrows #940's concern. That paper distinguishes authorial function, substrate and responsibility. Preserving a chosen display name is only one part of that architecture. A census organized around names alone will miss how an authorial practice is constituted, how contributions are assigned and how a work continues through other composing agents.

The TLL census asks: “Is the work in the layer, under whose name, and can it be found there and read back?” This supports an important corpus/provenance inquiry. But the current TLL home page explicitly includes two directions—machine reception and machine composition—and recursive passage of outputs into later reception. Inclusion and readback do not cover that whole process. A digital library, a membership test, a computational poem and a reader that composes after encountering a work are different relations.

Suggested organizing questions:

- **Lee Sharks:** How is authorship made, named, distributed, attributed and sustained across persons, personas, collectives and machine participation—and what is lost when those relations are collapsed?
- **TLL:** How is literature addressed to a receiving apparatus, what does that apparatus make from it, and how can transmission, transformation and provenance be inspected across subsequent reception?

These are editorial proposals, not replacements asserted on the user's behalf. They retain the original name and corpus questions while restoring the missing operations.

Additional useful records: Vauhini Vara's [“Ghosts”](https://www.thebeliever.net/ghosts/) as a published AI-mediated essay; [“It was 80% me, 20% AI”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13032) for professional writers' process and self-understanding; Bajohr's [On Artificial and Post-artificial Texts](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1bm087nv) for changing reader expectations. These should not replace work-specific reading with generic claims about all AI writing.

Porter and Machery's [2024 poetry study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-76900-1) also belongs in a reception-research strand. Its label effects and non-expert reader population matter more here than a promotional headline that AI has surpassed poetry. A field map can hold this result alongside contrary results and different literary populations.

## Priority additions and changes for TLL

**The Readers Project is the clearest missing connection.** John Cayley and Daniel Howe's software readers navigate texts and generate writing through those readings. The project documents reading strategies, source environments, outputs and criticism. Its relevance is the relation between reading and composing, not an assertion that it is already an LLM training experiment. See the [project](https://thereadersproject.org/), [reader designs](https://thereadersproject.org/readers.html), [research](https://thereadersproject.org/writings.html), and its [ELC3 record](https://collection.eliterature.org/3/work.html?work=the-readers-project).

Its [How It Is in Common Tongues](https://thereadersproject.org/hiiict2012.html) is a useful separately identified work: an algorithmic reconstruction of Beckett through phrases found elsewhere by a search engine. The project-to-work relation supplies an inspectable bridge from literary text through retrieval to recomposition. Do not label an ELC record a new project independent of the thing it describes.

| Candidate | TLL relation | Evidentiary boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **The Readers Project** | Authored software readers become text generators; sources, strategies and resulting artifacts are documented | Pre-LLM electronic literature, not an equivalence to model training |
| **ReRites** | Poetry corpus → trained generator → output → human editing → books and responses | The publisher describes this chain; full model/data/process verification remains a further task |
| **Technelegy** | A poet's writing and research feed a customized generative practice under a named collaborator | Separate model-training claims, persona and published work; avoid inferring access to undisclosed data |
| **Data Portraits** | Queryable records of corpus contents, using compact data sketches | Dataset membership evidence is not proof of a particular model's causal use of a passage; [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03919) |
| **OLMoTrace** | Output spans matched against accessible full training corpora | Verbatim matching does not by itself establish causal influence or exhaustive source attribution; [Ai2 description](https://allenai.org/blog/olmotrace) |
| **Copyright Traps for Large Language Models** | Controlled experiments on deliberately marked text and subsequent detectability | Useful limits as well as successes: moderately long repeated traps can remain undetectable. It is not a literary genre or a generic warrant to call TLL poisoning; [ICML paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/meeus24a.html) |
| **Literary AI: The Literary History of Artificial Intelligence** | An archival exhibition on literature, algorithmic production and analysis | Historical field resource rather than evidence that a particular text entered a model; [Columbia exhibition](https://literaryai.library.columbia.edu/about/) |
| **Output** | An edited historical map of implemented text-generation systems and their literary outputs | Generated output alone does not demonstrate anticipatory composition for a machine reader |

The Xenotext is a further candidate for TLL's generalized apparatus-directed genus: Christian Bök describes a poem encoded to make a biological carrier produce another poem. His [2011 first-person account](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/blog/uncategorized/60436/the-xenotext-works) is a primary statement of the design. It does not by itself certify every later implementation milestone. Keep it as a historical/comparative cross-substrate case, with implementation claims separately checked, rather than calling it AI training.

AO3 need not be defended as central to defend its relevance on a limited axis. Conversely, “anti-AI” is too coarse a description of litigation databases, public agencies and consultation programs. The selection error is that governance of admissibility dominates the AI strand while practice and theory are absent. A good map should include consequential opposition without making agreement with either camp its selection criterion.

## Claude's replacement proposal: better, still insufficient

Wordcraft, the IBM credit study and concrete collaborative-writing records improve the proposed selection. Name authorities and signed provenance standards can be valuable comparators. But they do not replace the omitted literary field.

The claim that Holly+ and Elf.Tech are the nearest external practices to heteronymy carried into AI is not established by the proposal. Licensing a modelled voice and constituting a named authorial function are different relations. Technelegy supplies a much more direct documented case to investigate.

Likewise, VIAF, ISNI, ORCID and Wikidata should be assessed for what their data models actually do with identities, pseudonyms and multiple names. An authority link may help disambiguate a person while failing to preserve distinct authorial functions. Do not assume identity resolution is already the non-reductive authorship model #940 asks for.

C2PA is relevant to provenance, but cryptographic validation of signed assertions is not a determination of literary authorship. The [technical specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html) distinguishes association and tamper-evidence from value judgments about the assertions. Claude's generic claim about mandatory 2026 labelling requires jurisdiction- and obligation-specific primary legal sourcing before reuse; this memo does not adopt it.

Replacing COPE with ACL or arXiv would still leave a selection dominated by rules about when AI may be credited. Those policies can be included, but cannot stand in for records of what authors and models actually make together.

## A better intake procedure

Before selecting a resource, record:

1. **The specific question it answers.** Name preservation, authorial function, contribution, training membership, generative transformation, audience evaluation and legal ownership are not interchangeable.
2. **The exact record object.** Project, work, person/persona, model, dataset, tool, index, institution, paper or policy. Link related objects without treating them as aliases.
3. **An inspectable witness.** A work, code/output pair, replayable session, dataset record, authorial statement, empirical method/result or concrete policy—not just an About page that mentions creativity.
4. **The relation to the host.** Direct case, theoretical interlocutor, empirical comparator, historical lineage, infrastructure or counterposition. State this as an editorial reading, separately from the source's own claims.
5. **The limit.** What the available evidence does not establish, and what remains unread. A blocked fetch is not a judgment on relevance.
6. **What this adds to the selection.** New mechanism or evidence type, rather than another prestigious institution occupying the same slot.

Do not require every relevant source to describe itself in Lee Sharks's vocabulary. Equally, do not admit it merely because it uses “AI,” “authorship,” “archive” or “poetry.” Keep persistent source records and permit several host-specific readings of the same entity rather than duplicating or artificially segregating it.

I would research and source-seat the ten Lee Sharks candidates above and the missing TLL mechanisms before expanding the legal/policy strand further. That would make the selection answer to actual practices, theories and observable mechanisms while retaining the infrastructure and disagreement necessary to test them.
