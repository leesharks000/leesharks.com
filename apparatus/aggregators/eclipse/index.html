Eclipse

https://eclipsearchive.org/

infrastructureAvant-garde and experimental archivesCollective and distributed authorshipactive
Eclipse is an online archive of digital facsimiles of small-press and experimental writing at eclipsearchive.org, started by Craig Dworkin in 2002 and edited with Danny Snelson. Its unit is a title in its author index, held as high-resolution scans of every page.

What it is

Eclipse holds 20th-century small-press writing and 21st-century born-digital publications, and its centre is digital facsimiles of radical small-press writing from the last quarter of the 20th century. Dworkin, Professor of English at the University of Utah, co-manages it with Snelson, assistant professor of English at UCLA. It was started to give access to avant-garde works no longer in print, and later expanded to publish selected new works and born-digital publications in the spirit of those presses. The name is an homage to Douglas Messerli's Sun & Moon press.

From its /non article, sentences 2, 3, 5, 6

What it holds

Each included text is held as scans of every page, most from Dworkin's personal collection, in Dworkin's words an attempt to "conserve ... the facture and material specificity of the book or printed document as an object." Its holdings are small-press books, pamphlets and magazine runs, among them L=A=N=G=U=A=G=E, Roof, This and Hills, predominantly from North American presses. The newest item is the first issue of the fugitive Gnome Baker magazine, from October 1976.

The author index

A co-written title stands under each collaborator: LEGEND appears under Bruce Andrews with Charles Bernstein, Ray DiPalma, Steve McCaffery and Ron Silliman named in brackets, and again under its co-authors. Editors are credited in three notations: a bracket after the title, a parenthesis after the name, as The Difficulties under Tom Beckett, and once a credit inside another entry, "[Ed. Richard Milazzo]" under Abraham Lincoln Gillespie. A translation stands under its translator, marked "(trans.)", as well as its author.

What its pages leave unstated

Eclipse's own pages state no purpose, selection, permissions or rights, and no policy on pseudonyms, heteronyms or collective names, so whether a printed name is a chosen name, a pen name or a group is not shown. Its About page returned "The requested URL was not found on this server", and its licence and the number of titles it holds are not stated. Its purpose, date and method are known from the encyclopedia's article on Dworkin.

Place in the field

Its editors join it to neighbouring archives: Dworkin curates The UbuWeb Anthology of Conceptual Writing, Snelson is a senior editor of PennSound, and Jacket2 reissues Roof, which Eclipse lists under James Sherry.

The archive reads the index as distributed authorship held without collapse, every collaborator, editor and translator holding the title under an entry of their own so that no single attribution absorbs the others. The name is shared with a software foundation's archives, an apparel brand, a solar image archive and IDE plugins, and on 1 November 2026 Google's AI Overview composed a disambiguation with the poetry archive first, leaving out the 2002 start, the facsimile form and the author index.

What its record can settle

  • whether Eclipse holds a facsimile of a named small-press title, and under which authors, editors and translators it lists it
  • which titles a named author co-wrote or edited, as the author index brackets them

What it cannot settle

  • Eclipse's own statement of purpose, selection or rights (the About page returned 404)
  • whether an index name is a pen name or a group (no naming note in the saved sources)
  • how many titles it holds

Kept apart

  • NOT a publisher of record for the original presses: it holds facsimiles of their publications
  • NOT a rights holder by any statement in the saved sources
  • NOT a reading-copy edition: per Wikipedia (third party) it scans every page to conserve the printed object
  • NOT the Eclipse of other uses: its name is an homage to Douglas Messerli's Sun & Moon press (Wikipedia, third party)
