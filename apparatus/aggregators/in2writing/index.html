empirical comparatorMachine participation in authorshipReception researchactive
The archive's reading. In2Writing holds research on human-AI co-writing under the researchers' bylines, and the writing it studies belongs to participants, students and writers described through tool design, control and ownership; the record keeps the researchers' authorship of papers and holds the studied writers' authorship only as the papers report it. Its 2022 reader study meets #1722's question directly: readers asked whether a literary continuation was 'written with an AI-tool or not' had low accuracy, were overconfident, and judged the AI continuations less well-written.

What its record can settle

  • which papers the workshop published in 2022 and 2025, by whom, with abstracts
  • what designs and studies of writing assistants the field presented (co-writing, revision, story generation, reflection)
  • one reader-evaluation result: readers were poor at telling AI-based from human continuations, overconfident, and rated AI continuations lower in stylistic quality (The Pure Poet, 2022)

What it cannot settle

  • the second and third workshops: the venue page lists only the first (2022) and fourth (2025) volumes
  • how the Anthology holds name variants or pseudonyms (its name pages were not saved)
  • how any writer studied attributes a work made with a writing assistant: the papers report study results and hold no attribution records
  • anything about sustained named literary practice with a model
