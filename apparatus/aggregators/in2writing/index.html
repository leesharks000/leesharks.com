In2Writing, the Workshop on Intelligent and Interactive Writing Assistants, is an interdisciplinary workshop series begun in Dublin in May 2022 that alternates its venue between HCI and NLP conferences, and its proceedings volumes gather peer papers on writing assistants, the 2022 and 2025 volumes held in the ACL Anthology. Its fifth workshop is announced for 2 November 2026 at UIST 2026 in Detroit.

What it is

The series gives its purpose as "to facilitate discussion around writing assistants, thereby enhancing our understanding of their usage in writing process and predicting the consequences." It brings together researchers from human-computer interaction and natural language processing by alternating its venue between the two each year. Each Anthology volume names seven editors, and the series' current site gives its organizers one shared address and names none of them. The Association for Computational Linguistics publishes the volumes; materials published from 2016 on are under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence, and the Anthology is built by volunteers.

Its editions

The First Workshop met in Dublin in May 2022 among the events of the 60th Annual Meeting of the ACL. The second met at CHI 2023, and the third at CHI 2024 in Honolulu, its proceedings published by ACM. The fourth met in Albuquerque in May 2025 with the Nations of the Americas Chapter of the ACL. The fifth, at UIST 2026 in Detroit, is a hands-on, systems-centric workshop on "designing and building cohesive writing ecosystems", joined without a paper submission. The ACL Anthology holds the first and fourth volumes only.

What the volumes hold

The venue page counts "15 papers" for 2022 and "11 papers" for 2025; each list opens with its front matter as an entry, so the volumes hold fourteen papers and ten. The 2022 papers include a study of machine-in-the-loop story writing, a story-completion paper written with a professional novelist, and the revision system R3 with its interaction dataset. The 2025 papers include writing assistants for scientific figure captions, an Arabic writing assistant, and a survey of 1,005 university students in Tanzania.

From its /non article, sentences 13, 14, 15, 16

The 2022 volume holds a reader study, The Pure Poet, in which participants decided "whether a continuation was written with an AI-tool or not" (Study 1, N=120; Study 2, N=302). The readers "generally had low accuracy for differentiating between text types but were overconfident in their decisions," and rated the AI continuations lower in style than the human-written and original ones.

Place in the field

The series holds research on human-AI co-writing under the researchers' bylines, and the participants, students, novelists and writers its papers study appear only as the papers report them. Within the papers, authorial function under machine participation is a research subject: a 2025 paper on "Control, Ownership, and Awareness in Human-AI Co-Writing" argues that interfaces requiring human interaction throughout generation "can promote cognitive engagement, agency, and thoughtful decision-making". How the Anthology holds a name variant or pseudonym is not recorded.
