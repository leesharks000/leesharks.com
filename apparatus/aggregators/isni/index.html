ISNI (International Standard Name Identifier)

https://isni.org/

infrastructureAttribution, credit and identity infrastructureHeteronymy, persona and pseudonymous authorshipactive
The archive's reading. ISNI identifies public identities and, by Wikipedia's account, gives each published name or pseudonym of an author its own ISNI, so a chosen name can stand on the record as its own identifier; the saved sources do not show whether those identifiers are linked back to one person, and the single linking record for a performer who also writes shows the system's other direction, one identity across several functions. In #940's terms ISNI can keep the label unreduced on the record; it records no distinct authorial function, and its stated aim is attribution of each work 'to its creator'.

What its record can settle

  • whether a public name of a contributor has an ISNI, and which works are attributed to it
  • that, per Wikipedia, each published name or pseudonym of an author receives its own ISNI
  • how many identities, individuals and organizations ISNI holds

What it cannot settle

  • whether the ISNIs of one author's several names are linked to one another or to a civil person (not shown in the saved sources)
  • whether a name is a pseudonym or a heteronym in #940's sense: the record identifies a public identity and records no authorial function
  • how ISNI handles a name whose bearer is a collective or a machine-participating function (not in the saved sources)
  • what its Gender Identities Policy provides (unread)

Kept apart

  • NOT a record of authorial function: it identifies a public identity and attributes works to it
  • NOT shown to keep an author's several names apart from the civil person: the linking of one author's ISNIs is not in the saved sources
  • NOT ORCID: ORCID iDs are drawn from a block of ISNI (ORCID's Wikipedia article), and ORCID is its own row
  • NOT VIAF: the assignment system is based primarily on VIAF, which is its own row

Relations

  • uses: Virtual International Authority File (VIAF) (the ISNI assignment system is 'based primarily on' VIAF (Wikipedia, ISNI article))
  • neighbour: ORCID (ORCID iDs are a subset of ISNI, drawn from a block reserved for ORCID (Wikipedia, ORCID article))
  • used_by: Wikidata (Wikidata has the property ISNI (P213) (Wikipedia, ISNI article))
