The archive's reading. A measured instance of the discount #1722 names, at the level of credit: for equivalent contributions, these participants assign less to the machine partner than to a human one, and they want the machine's involvement disclosed. It studies how credit is apportioned between contributors; it says nothing of how a declared machine-witness function or a chosen name is held, which #940 asks.

What its record can settle

  • how a surveyed population of knowledge workers assigns credit to AI and to human partners for equivalent contributions to writing and editing
  • which contribution features (type, amount, initiative) shift the credit they assign
  • whether those participants think disclosing AI involvement matters

What it cannot settle

  • who deserves authorship of a given work: the study records participants' judgments
  • how literary readers, writers or other populations assign credit
  • how credit is recorded under a name, pseudonym or heteronym
  • the survey instrument, scenarios and effect sizes (in the full paper, unsaved)

Kept apart

  • NOT a normative account of who deserves authorship: it records participants' judgments
  • NOT a study of literary authorship: the participants are knowledge workers and the tasks writing and editing
  • NOT a record of works or names: it records attitudes about credit
  • NOT tied to the AI Attribution Toolkit in the saved sources: no link between the two is shown
