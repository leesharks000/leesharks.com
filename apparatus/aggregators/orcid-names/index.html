How authorship is made and named: a meta-aggregator / ORCID

ORCID

https://orcid.org/

infrastructureAttribution, credit and identity infrastructureactive
ORCID, the Open Researcher and Contributor ID, is a nonproprietary persistent identifier for the authors and contributors of scholarly communication, issued free of charge to researchers with a record connected to it. It is run by ORCID, Inc., a not-for-profit incorporated in Delaware in August 2010 and sustained by fees from its member organizations, and its registry launched on 16 October 2012.

What it is

Its registry holds unique persistent identifiers for researchers and connects them with their contributions; the identifier provides "a persistent identity for humans", and its stated scope is scholarly communication. An iD is a URL of sixteen characters, the last a check digit that may be the letter X. ORCID hosts the record and its holder edits it, choosing which works it includes and who can see them.

From its /non article, sentences 2, 3, 4, 39, 36

The names a record holds

The identifier answers the problem that most personal names are not unique, change, differ in order across cultures and are written in different writing systems. The iD separates its holder from others of the same name and from variations of the holder's own name, such as initials, maiden names and transliterations, and a record can contain other versions of its holder's name. The iD is tied to an individual's name, and under ORCID's own terms iDs are issued to individual natural persons only. How a record holds a pseudonym or a heteronym as such is the subject of ORCID's support article on names, which refused the census's fetcher and stays unread.

From its /non article, sentences 7, 9, 10, 11, 32, 12

Origin, operator and adoption

ORCID was announced in 2009 as a collaborative effort by publishers "to resolve the author name ambiguity problem in scholarly communication". ORCID, Inc. is governed by a board representative of its membership, and its founding principles commit it to "reliable attribution of authors and contributors" and to a yearly release of its public data under a CC0 waiver.

From its /non article, sentences 33, 34, 46

ORCID iDs are a subset of the International Standard Name Identifier, so that one person may hold both, "effectively, two ISNIs". ORCID reported 14,727,479 live accounts as of 2 August 2022. Some publishers and funders require an iD, and DataCite and Crossref update ORCID records when an iD is found in newly registered DOI names.

What its record can settle

  • whether two scholarly works are credited to the same registered contributor, through one identifier
  • that the identifier answers the name-ambiguity problem of non-unique, changing and variously ordered personal names (as the article states it)
  • how many accounts and members ORCID reported in 2022

What it cannot settle

  • how an ORCID record holds a pseudonym, a heteronym or several published names as such, beyond the third-party statement that a record can contain other versions of the holder's name (the support page is unread)
  • whether an identifier corresponds to one civil person or to an authorial function
  • who wrote a work, or what each contributor did
  • anything outside scholarly communication

Kept apart

  • NOT ORCID's own account throughout: the row rests mainly on the Wikipedia article, a third party, because ORCID's support page returned http 403; ORCID's own About page supplies its self-description, governance, founding principles and licence
  • NOT a record of how pseudonyms or heteronyms are held as such: the record can carry other versions of the holder's name (NIH, third party), and the source that would show how a pseudonym is held is unread
  • NOT a record of authorial function or contribution roles: it resolves contributor identity
  • NOT a register of literary authorship generally: its stated scope is scholarly communication
