How authorship is made and named: a meta-aggregator / ORCID

ORCID

https://orcid.org/

infrastructureAttribution, credit and identity infrastructureactive
The archive's reading. As the third-party article describes it, ORCID binds contributions to 'a persistent identity for humans', which resolves the person behind several names; whether that resolution keeps a chosen name as the author's own or reduces it to the civil person is the open question #940 asks of identity infrastructure, and the source that would answer it, ORCID's own page on names, was refused. The article's identity model is disambiguation of persons, and it records nothing of a distinct authorial function.

What its record can settle

  • whether two scholarly works are credited to the same registered contributor, through one identifier
  • that the identifier answers the name-ambiguity problem of non-unique, changing and variously ordered personal names (as the article states it)
  • how many accounts and members ORCID reported in 2022

What it cannot settle

  • how an ORCID record holds a pseudonym, a heteronym or several published names (the support page is unread)
  • whether an identifier corresponds to one civil person or to an authorial function
  • who wrote a work, or what each contributor did
  • anything outside scholarly communication

Kept apart

  • NOT ORCID's own account: the row rests on the Wikipedia article, a third party, because ORCID's support page returned http 403
  • NOT a record of how pseudonyms or heteronyms are held: the source that would show it is unread
  • NOT a record of authorial function or contribution roles: it resolves contributor identity
  • NOT a register of literary authorship generally: its stated scope is scholarly communication
