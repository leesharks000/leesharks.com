empirical comparatorMachine participation in authorshipAttribution, credit and identity infrastructurehistorical
The archive's reading. The workshop publishes each story under its writer's name, keeps the writers identified by initials in the paper for the sake of their published work, and records workflows ranging from largely generated stories to stories mostly written by their authors; writers feared 'reputational harm' from unknown sources of suggestions. The archive reads it as machine participation declared at the level of the tool and the cohort, with authorship held by the named writer, and as a provider-run record whose own notice separates Google from the content.

What its record can settle

  • how thirteen named professional writers report using a LaMDA-based editor, and how much generated text each workflow admitted, by initials
  • under whose names the stories are published and on what consent
  • what the writers reported on voice, style and the unknown origin of suggestions

What it cannot settle

  • which sentences of a given story LaMDA generated
  • how writers outside this commissioned, provider-organized cohort work
  • the source of any suggestion in LaMDA's training data

Kept apart

  • NOT authored by LaMDA: the stories stand under their writers' names
  • NOT a neutral census of writers: a provider-organized, commissioned cohort of 13
  • NOT a record of which text was generated: workflows are reported by writer, without per-sentence marking
  • NOT endorsed by Google in content: the site's own notice

  • neighbour: rerites (Allison Parrish, a workshop writer, writes a response essay in ReRites)
