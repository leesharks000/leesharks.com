AI-generated poetry is indistinguishable from human-written poetry and is rated more favorably

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-76900-1

empirical comparatorReception researchhistorical
The archive's reading. Two results sit together here: told that a poem is AI-generated, non-expert readers rate it lower, the label effect #1722 meets as a discount on origin; and untold, the same readers take the generated poems for human ones and read the human poets' complexity as machine incoherence. The population is non-expert and the generated poems unselected; the result bears on how a stated origin shifts judgment, and its title's claim of indistinguishability holds for these readers and these poems.

What its record can settle

  • whether non-expert, US-based readers can tell ChatGPT 3.5 poems from poems by these ten poets (below chance, in this study)
  • whether being told a poem is AI-generated lowers non-expert ratings (it did, for most qualities)
  • which features those readers used to judge authorship, as the authors analyse them

What it cannot settle

  • whether AI poetry is better than human poetry: the ratings are non-expert preferences
  • how expert readers or poets judge the same poems
  • how authorship of a poem is constituted or named
  • anything about later models or later readers' expectations (the authors' own stated limit)

Kept apart

  • NOT evidence that AI has surpassed poetry: the ratings are non-expert readers' preferences
  • NOT a study of expert readers: 90.4% of participants read poetry a few times per year or less
  • NOT a study of human-AI co-writing: the generated poems are human out of the loop
  • NOT a record of authorial names or personas
  • NOT generalized by its authors beyond current models and current beliefs

Relations

  • neighbour: The human-authorship halo: attribution bias in literary style evaluation by humans and AI (both test how a stated human or AI label shifts readers' judgment of literary texts; the halo abstract does not cite Porter and Machery)
