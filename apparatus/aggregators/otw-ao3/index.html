Archive of Our Own

https://archiveofourown.org/

peripheral comparatorHeteronymy, persona and pseudonymous authorshipAttribution, credit and identity infrastructureCollective and distributed authorshippaused
The archive's reading. AO3 holds each work under a pseud its author chose, several pseuds to one account, with no legal name shown in what was read, and it holds works about real people (RPF) as fanworks. The archive reads the pseud-to-account link as a platform rule on one axis of the question, a chosen name kept as the author's own and answerable through the account, close to #940's attribution without reduction; in #940's terms the pseud changes the label, the sources show no distinct authorial function constituted under it, and they show no machine participation.

What its record can settle

  • under which pseud a fanwork was posted, and with what tags its author gave it
  • which fandoms and how many works the archive holds
  • on what terms the OTW holds fanworks (nonprofit, fair use, noncommercial)

What it cannot settle

  • the civil identity behind a pseud (the record keeps the pseud linked to an account)
  • the legal status of any given work (the fair-use position is the OTW legal team's belief)
  • anything during the maintenance closure from 29 October 2026, when the site could not be read

Kept apart

  • NOT a social network: it defines itself primarily as an archive
  • NOT a commercial platform: works are expected to be noncommercial
  • NOT a register of authors' legal names: works stand under pseuds
  • NOT readable during the maintenance closure from 29 October 2026

Relations

  • neighbour: Fanlore (both are projects of the Organization for Transformative Works (Wikipedia's OTW article); the GeoCities Rescue Project moved content into both)
