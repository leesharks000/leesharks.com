The toolkit records contribution and accountability as the creator declares them, the declared-provenance condition #940 sets for an authorial function, and #1722's distinction holds here: a declaration of substrate is offered as provenance, and the toolkit makes no verdict on it. It records process and accountability for one work; it holds no name, so it neither keeps a chosen name nor reduces it to a civil one.

What its record can settle

  • what a creator says AI contributed to a given work, along the toolkit's dimensions, when the creator has used it and attached the statement
  • how one research prototype divides a human-AI workflow into disclosable contribution, modification and accountability

What it cannot settle

  • whether a statement is true: the toolkit takes the creator's word and verifies nothing
  • whether a work was made with AI when no statement is attached (it detects nothing)
  • under what name the work stands or who its author is
  • how readers receive the statements (no reception data in the saved sources)
  • who built it (no operator is named in the saved sources)

Kept apart

  • NOT a detector of AI use: it 'centers human judgment and self reporting' and infers nothing automatically
  • NOT a verification of provenance: the statement records what the creator reports
  • NOT a legal or compliance instrument: it avoids that framing by design
  • NOT a registry of statements or works: the saved sources describe no store
  • NOT a record of authorial names
