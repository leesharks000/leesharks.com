The archive's reading. The Office holds authorship for human authors and decides, claim by claim, how much of a work a human made, requiring the AI-generated part to be disclaimed; the saved page records its decisions by the work's title and says nothing on the name an author chooses. The archive reads it as the legal register of #940's civil-name regime (copyright attaching to legal names) and as the boundary where machine participation is attributed by subtraction: the machine's part is disclaimed and the human remainder is registered as the work's authorship.

What its record can settle

  • what the Office has published on the copyrightability of outputs created using generative AI, and when
  • how the Office decided the registration claims for SURYAST, Théâtre D'opéra Spatial, Zarya of the Dawn and A Recent Entrance to Paradise
  • what position the government took in Thaler v. Perlmutter through the appeal and the certiorari stage

What it cannot settle

  • how the Office registers a work under a pseudonym or heteronym (not on the saved page)
  • how courts outside the United States treat AI-generated works
  • whether any particular unregistered work would be registered

Kept apart

  • NOT a court: its registration and Review Board decisions are administrative, and the courts' decisions in Thaler v. Perlmutter are linked as litigation documents
  • NOT a statute: the guidance is a policy statement and the Report a policy study
  • NOT a statement on pseudonyms or heteronyms: the saved page has none
  • NOT finished: Part 3 is a pre-publication version
