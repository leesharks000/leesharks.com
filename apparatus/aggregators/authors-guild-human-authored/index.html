Human Authored Certification

https://authorsguild.org/human-authored/

counterpositionBoundary and governanceactive
Human Authored is the Authors Guild's certification of books whose text a human wrote: an author certifies a book under licence, may then carry the Guild's certification mark on it, and has the title listed under its own registration number in a public database. It opened in beta for Guild members in January 2025, and on 2 March 2026 the Guild announced its expansion to every author whose books are published in the United States.

What it certifies

The definition is centred on the text. On the program page the text of the book 'was written by a human and not generated by AI', minimal uses such as spelling and grammar checks or research excepted; the FAQ, the usage guidelines and the announcement require it to be 'fully authored by one or more human beings', a de minimis amount excepted, a threshold the FAQ ties to the U.S. Copyright Office's guidance. AI used for an index, a title or research does not disqualify a work, and AI that rewrites full sentences is not acceptable.

From its /non article, sentences 8, 9, 11, 12

Who certifies, and how

Authors of books published in the United States may certify wherever they live. Every author signs a licensing agreement for each title; non-members verify their identity through Veriff and pay $10 a title, while members certify at no cost. The program page waives verification for members, and the FAQ gives the ground: members are verified when they join the Guild. More than 3,000 authors had certified 5,000 titles by the CEO's count to Publishers Weekly.

From its /non article, sentences 14, 17, 18, 7

Enforcement and its stated limit

The author warrants that the work is Human Authored, and a licensee who uses the mark on AI-generated text is in breach of the licence and liable for trademark infringement. The Guild states that it knows no truly reliable way to test a work for AI-generated material and vets no manuscript. Jane Friedman found certification on the honor system problematic; the Guild answered that self-certification is standard in publishing. A lack of results in the database means only that a work has not been registered, 'not that it is generated by AI'.

From its /non article, sentences 25, 26, 29, 30, 33

Place in the field

Beside it stand Created by Humans, from June 2024, Books by People in the United Kingdom, from 2025, and the Society of Authors' scheme. The mark's trademark status is stated two ways: registered in the FAQ, a pending application elsewhere.

The archive's reading

The census reads the certificate as setting authorship's boundary at the human writing of a text and admitting machine participation only as minimal uses, so that where machine participation is constitutive of a work its definition leaves no certifiable place; the saved page leaves open whether it carries the name the author chose. The archive's packet on provenance calls the label 'a market-category signal, not a complete theory of authorship', and answers its binary frame: 'Authorship is not a purity state. It is an accountability structure.' False accusations of AI use between authors are the case the archive names the existence tax.

What its record can settle

  • whether a named book carries a Human Authored certification, and its registration number
  • on what terms an author may certify a book (fee, identity verification, licensing agreement)

What it cannot settle

  • whether a certified text was in fact written without AI (the record is the author's certification; the FAQ states that the Guild knows no truly reliable test and that the program is self-certification with enforcement)
  • whether an author may certify under a pen name or heteronym (not on the program page, the FAQ, the usage guidelines or the announcement)
  • anything about books published outside the United States, beyond the FAQ's referral of books published in the United Kingdom to the Society of Authors

Kept apart

  • NOT a test or detection of AI text: it records the author's own certification, and the Guild vets no manuscript before certifying
  • NOT a copyright registration
  • NOT limited to Authors Guild members: non-members may certify for $10 per title with identity verification
  • NOT a certification of the whole book: it covers the text
  • NOT the database alone: the row's object is the certification policy, whose public database and mark are parts of it
