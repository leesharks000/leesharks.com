counterpositionBoundary and governanceactive
The archive's reading. The archive reads the certificate as an explicit counterposition that asserts authorship's boundary at the human writing of a text, admits machine participation only as 'minimal uses', and binds the non-member's claim to an identity check. It answers #1722's verdict of fakeness for a work with the author's own certification; where machine participation is constitutive of the work, its definition leaves no certifiable place, and the saved page does not say whether the certificate carries the name the author chose.

What its record can settle

  • whether a named book carries a Human Authored certification, and its registration number
  • on what terms an author may certify a book (fee, identity verification, licensing agreement)

What it cannot settle

  • whether a certified text was in fact written without AI (the record is the author's certification; no verification of the text is stated)
  • whether an author may certify under a pen name or heteronym (not on the saved page)
  • anything about books published outside the United States

Kept apart

  • NOT a test or detection of AI text: it records the author's own certification
  • NOT a copyright registration
  • NOT limited to Authors Guild members: non-members may certify for $10 per title with identity verification
  • NOT a certification of the whole book: it covers the text
  • NOT the database alone: the row's object is the certification policy, whose public database and mark are parts of it
