How authorship is made and named: a meta-aggregator / Virtual International Authority File (VIAF)

Virtual International Authority File (VIAF)

https://viaf.org/

infrastructureAttribution, credit and identity infrastructureMeta-aggregationactive
The archive's reading. VIAF, as Wikipedia describes it, resolves a name across library authority files by linking records judged identical into one cluster, with see and see-also forms carried from the source records; that is identity resolution led by libraries, and the saved source does not show whether a pseudonym or heteronym stays a distinct cluster or folds into its bearer's. Clusters that 'coalesce or split' mean an authorial name can be merged with another by algorithm, the collapse #940 names, or separated again; which happens to a given name is not recorded here.

What its record can settle

  • which participating authority files hold records for a name, as linked into one cluster
  • what VIAF is and who runs it, as a third party describes it
  • that cluster identifiers can change as clusters coalesce or split

What it cannot settle

  • how VIAF holds a pseudonym or heteronym: as a see or see-also form of the bearer's cluster, or as a cluster of its own (not in the saved source)
  • whether a given chosen name has stayed distinct or been merged by the clustering algorithm
  • how many clusters or records it holds (not in the saved source)
  • anything from VIAF's own pages (refused)

Kept apart

  • NOT VIAF's own account: the row rests on the Wikipedia article, a third party, because viaf.org returned http 403
  • NOT shown to keep a pseudonym or heteronym as a distinct entity: the saved source says nothing of pseudonyms
  • NOT a stable identifier in every case: clusters coalesce or split monthly
  • NOT led by authors: it is led by libraries (Borgman, quoted by Wikipedia)
  • NOT ISNI: ISNI is a participant and its assignment system is based on VIAF; it is its own row

Relations
