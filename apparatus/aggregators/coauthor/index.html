CoAuthor is a research dataset of human-AI collaborative writing by Mina Lee, Percy Liang and Qian Yang (Stanford University and Cornell University), published with a CHI 2022 paper. It holds the interactions between 63 writers and four instances of GPT-3 across 1445 writing sessions in English, each session recorded at the keystroke level so that it can be replayed.

What it is

Its paper presents it as "a dataset designed for revealing GPT-3's capabilities in assisting creative and argumentative writing." In each session a writer received a prompt and an instance of GPT-3, wrote freely, asked the model for suggestions, accepted or dismissed them, and edited the accepted suggestions or earlier text in any order. The tab key brought suggestions in a text editor whose code is published. The instances are GPT-3's davinci engine at two randomness settings, high (temperature 0.75) and low (temperature 0.3).

From its /non article, sentences 2, 4, 5, 6

What it holds

Every interaction was recorded at the keystroke level with timestamps, "so that we could replay them precisely," and the dataset is published with an interface for replaying the sessions. The creative writing holds 830 stories by 58 writers and the argumentative writing 615 essays by 49 writers, written to prompts from the WritingPrompts subreddit and The New York Times. Stories and essays run to 418 words, a session averages 11.8 queries, and 72.3% of suggestions were accepted.

From its /non article, sentences 7, 8, 9

The writers were "Qualified crowd workers from Amazon Mechanical Turk." The browse page lists each session by type, prompt, randomness, writer and session ID, the writer held by a short identifier such as "A1198".

From its /non article, sentences 10, 11

Authorship in the record

The dataset divides its text by origin, "Percentage of text written by humans: 72.6%", and one of its tutorials is for "Identifying authors of sentences". The paper reads the model's part as contribution, revealing it as a writing "collaborator" under various definitions of good collaboration. In the paper as its overview reports it, writers felt stronger ownership when they authored a larger proportion of the final text (r=0.3), and the amount of editing showed none. Amazon announced that Mechanical Turk, the platform the writers were recruited from, would close on 30 September 2026.

What its record can settle

  • how GPT-3's contribution to a writing session can be read from recorded interactions, under the paper's definitions of good collaboration
  • how many writers, model instances and sessions the dataset holds
  • who the writers were as a class (qualified crowd workers from Amazon Mechanical Turk) and how they are listed (short identifiers)
  • what share of the text the dataset gives as written by humans (72.6%), and how a sentence's author can be identified from the log

What it cannot settle

  • the writers' names: the browse page lists short identifiers only
  • how a sustained named poetic practice works: the study population and task setting differ
  • the dataset's license (not stated on the saved pages)

Kept apart

  • NOT a record of named authors: the dataset lists its writers, crowd workers from Amazon Mechanical Turk, by short identifiers
  • NOT a sustained literary practice: writing sessions in a study setting
  • NOT the model's authorship: the paper reads GPT-3 as 'collaborator' under definitions it sets
