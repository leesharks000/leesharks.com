Wikidata is a free, collaborative, multilingual and secondary knowledge base of structured data, hosted and owned by the Wikimedia Foundation, developed mainly by Wikimedia Deutschland and launched on 29 October 2012. Its unit is the item, a persistent QID with a label, a description, aliases and statements of property and value; its main page counted 123,855,402 data entities on 1 November 2026.

What it is

It is the central store of structured data for the Wikimedia projects and is open to others. Editors and bots enter its data. It calls itself secondary because it records statements with their sources and their connections to other databases. Its introduction publishes the data under the CC0 dedication, and its page footer confines CC0 to structured data in the main, Property, Lexeme and EntitySchema namespaces.

From its /non article, sentences 2, 4, 11, 12

How it holds statements

The property "author" can take as its value a person such as Sir Arthur Conan Doyle. Qualifiers and references are optional parts of a statement, and each statement has a rank: normal, preferred, or deprecated. It is limited to basic statements, such as when and where a work was published and who wrote it, much like a catalog record.

From its /non article, sentences 8, 9, 24

Identifiers and the pseudonym property

Wikidata has identifier properties for ISNI (P213), VIAF (P214) and ORCID (P496), incorporates VIAF file numbers, and crosswalks one identifier to another. Property P742, pseudonym, is "alias used by someone", and its aliases include pen name, stage name, user name and heteronym; its value is a string of up to 100 characters on the item of a human, character, group of humans or another allowed subject. Its examples pair a person with a name: Elsa Bernstein with Ernst Rosmer, Madonna with Louise Ciccone.

From its /non article, sentences 21, 20, 25, 26

The heteronym as an item of its own

Heteronyms are also held as items of their own. In April 2026 most of the Pessoa heteronyms on Wikidata were items described as "heteronym used by Fernando Pessoa", their statements mostly carrying no reference. Between 22 April and 20 May 2026 an account declared as the archive editor's created about 155 items, among them Johannes Sigil (Q139796042) and thirty-three heteronyms of Pessoa. Within the archive the model is read both as "closer to correct" (#96) and as a flattening of Pessoa's typology into one class (#668).

From its /non article, sentences 27, 29, 28

Admission is governed by a notability policy. On 20 December 2025 the items for the archive's heteronyms were deleted after a notification applying a test of a Wikipedia article, a structural need, or identifiers with serious sources, and on 4 January 2026 the item Q137446960, Johannes Sigil, was deleted as an "Empty item".

The archive's readings

In the terms of #940, a pseudonym changes the label attached to an authorial function and a heteronym changes the organization of the function itself. On the census's reading, Wikidata holds a chosen name as data about its bearer and records no authorial function, and P742's aliases treat pseudonym and heteronym as one. Asked for the name on 1 November 2026, Google's AI Overview was accurate as a data model and query service and left out the identifier hub and the pseudonym property.

The census record

The archive's reading. Wikidata holds a chosen name as data about its bearer: the pseudonym property P742 lists 'heteronym' among its aliases with pen name and stage name and stores the name as a string on the item of the person who used it, so in #940's terms the data model keeps the label and records it as a pseudonym of one function. The census's saved sources do not show whether a heteronym is given an item of its own or how such an item would stand to its civil bearer. The archive's own record does: in April 2026 most of the Pessoa heteronyms on Wikidata were items described as 'heteronym used by Fernando Pessoa', their statements mostly carrying no reference (#668); #96 (May 2026) reads Pessoa's heteronym as a first-class node of type heteronym linked to Pessoa by a creator property, 'closer to correct'; between 22 April and 20 May 2026 an account declared as the archive editor's created heteronym items, among them Johannes Sigil (Q139796042), and thirty-three heteronyms of Pessoa (#761); and the archive's minting procedure for a heteronymic position declares the position heteronymic in the description of its Wikidata item (#1009). On that record P742 is one of two ways Wikidata holds a chosen name: a string on the bearer's item, or an item of its own.

What its record can settle

  • what sourced statements Wikidata holds about a person or work
  • under which pseudonyms (P742) a person is recorded, with the qualifiers given
  • which identifiers in other authority files (ISNI, VIAF, ORCID) an item carries
  • how its data model holds a pen name or heteronym: as a string on the bearer's item

What it cannot settle

  • whether a name is a pseudonym or a heteronym in #940's sense: P742's aliases treat the two as one
  • whether a heteronym has an item of its own, and how it links to its bearer (not in the census's saved sources; the archive's deposits record such items, in ls_reading)
  • whether any given statement is sourced: references are optional and citation on P742 is a suggestion
  • how authorial function, voice or responsibility is distributed in a work

Kept apart

  • NOT a record of heteronymy as a distinct authorial function in P742: 'heteronym' is an alias of the pseudonym property, and the value is a string on the bearer's item
  • NOT a primary source: it calls itself a secondary knowledge base
  • NOT sourced by requirement: references are optional, and citation on P742 is a suggestion
  • NOT the field row wikidata-pseudonym: that row reads property P742 alone and stands outside the census order as a field reference; this row is Wikidata whole, with P742 recorded in it

Relations

  • uses: Virtual International Authority File (VIAF) (VIAF file numbers are incorporated into Wikidata (Wikipedia, VIAF article); Wikidata is also listed as a VIAF participant)
  • uses: ISNI (International Standard Name Identifier) (Wikidata has the property ISNI (P213) (Wikipedia, ISNI article))
  • neighbour: ORCID (Wikidata includes pages with ORCID identifiers (Wikipedia, ORCID article))
