Creative AI Tools & Resources Database (Creative AI Lab)

https://creative-ai.org/

The Creative AI Tools & Resources Database is an ongoing collection of tools and resources for artists, designers, engineers, curators and researchers who bring machine learning and AI into their practice, produced by the Creative AI Lab, a research partnership of Serpentine and the Department of Digital Humanities at King's College London. Launched on 9 July 2020 and published at creative-ai.org, it lists each tool under its developer and each resource under its authors.

What it is

The Lab gives itself as a space, since 2019, for long-term research by Serpentine Arts Technologies and the King's College department into artistic practices working with AI and machine learning. Its stated aim is to surface the 'back-end' knowledge that artistic practice holds about these tools and to overcome 'black-box' narratives. Its funders are listed as King's College London, Serpentine Galleries, the Arts and Humanities Research Council and Google's Artists + Machine Intelligence Grant.

From its /non article, sentences 3, 4, 8

What it holds

Its feed holds 271 entries, 148 tools and 123 resources, created between 23 June 2020 and 9 May 2024, none of them in 2022. Tools are compiled for their potential to bring new capabilities to artistic workflow, and a tool may be core to the production of the work itself or play a smaller assistive role.

From its /non article, sentences 10, 11

Each listing carries a column for its maker: tools are headed 'Developed by' and resources 'Author(s)'. Of the 148 tools 144 name a maker, and of the 123 resources 116, as a company or laboratory (OpenAI for Sora, Declare Lab for Mustango), as persons (Seth Forsgren and Hayk Martiros for Riffusion), and for resources as authors with a publisher. The collection is stated to be incomplete, a growing collection of research collected and commissioned by the Lab.

From its /non article, sentences 13, 16

How it was made

A list of 65 essential tools contributed by the curator and researcher Luba Elliott was a highlight of the launch, and the Lab's timeline records the launch 'with Luba Elliot', so spelled. Tool entries after 2023 are evaluated in their descriptions on five criteria: transparency, creative malleability, technical interoperability, calibre of research and propensity to redistribute.

From its /non article, sentences 7, 15

The programme beside it

The Lab also runs workshops, public discussions, writing sessions and online tutorials called R&D Labyrinths. One discussion, with Martin Zeilinger on 15 November 2021, addressed the impact of artificial intelligence on human-centered notions of creative agency, authorship and ownership. Its position paper on 'Creative AI' was published through the database in November 2022 (Serpentine lists it as of 2023), and Future Art Ecosystems 4, in March 2024, is the latest item of its timeline. The Lab is listed as ongoing; the feed's latest entry is of 9 May 2024.

The archive's reading. The database records tools and resources for making with machine learning, each under its maker, a developer for a tool and authors for a resource, and keeps no record of the works made with them or of how their authorship is attributed. What its record can settle

  • that the Lab launched a database of creative AI tools and resources on 9 July 2020, and who runs and funds it
  • that it records a maker for each listing, 'Developed by' for a tool and 'Author(s)' for a resource
  • how tools are chosen and, after 2023, evaluated (five criteria)
  • that its launch list of 65 tools was contributed by a named curator
  • what the Lab's research questions are (AI/ML tools and artistic practice, curatorial change, critical practice, aesthetics)

What it cannot settle

  • which entries the database now holds, or how many: the entries load from the site's feed, which was not saved
  • whose works were made with those tools, or under what name
  • how authorship of AI-made art is attributed in the Lab's commissions
  • whether the database is still added to: the saved sources say 'Ongoing', and the Lab's timeline ends in March 2024

Kept apart

  • NOT read entry by entry: the database's pages at creative-ai.org give its description, categories and column heads, and its entries load from a feed that was not saved
  • NOT a record of works or of their authors: it records the makers of the tools and resources it lists ('Developed by', 'Author(s)')
  • NOT the Creative AI Lab as a whole: the Lab's events, tutorials and position paper are its wider programme
  • NOT independent of the Lab: King's College London and Serpentine are the Lab's partners; HOLO is the third party
