The NEXT is the online museum, library and preservation space of born-digital literature kept by the Electronic Literature Organization, managed by the Electronic Literature Lab at Washington State University Vancouver, with Dene Grigar as Managing Director and Curator. Its unit is the work held in one of its 48 collections: author collections, online journals, anthologies, showcases, scholarly publications and the ELO's own historical media.

What it is

The NEXT describes itself as "a combination museum, library, and preservation space" that "maintains and makes its archives accessible for the next generation." Its mission is "maintaining and sharing the cultural record of human expression" from what Jay David Bolter calls the "late age of print" to the digital age. The Electronic Literature Organization lists it among its Electronic Literature Archives. A grant from The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation seeded it, and the ELO, WSU Vancouver and the Lab now support it.

From its /non article, sentences 1, 2, 5, 7

History

The sources date it differently. Wikipedia gives 2011 as the year established, and its body gives 2011 as the year the lab opened officially. Production teams worked on The NEXT from January to May 2021, a WSU report of 2 September 2021 is titled "Students create virtual museum of digital literature", and its exhibition afterflash is listed on 24 May 2021.

From its /non article, sentences 9, 10, 11

Holdings and preservation

The home page counts 48 collections; the museum first housed 30 collections of 2,500 works and held over 3,000 works by 2022. Its project description states that The NEXT "does not attempt to act as gate-keeper that defines value of a work of art" and sets a limit: "we cannot archive everything". Its own curatorial statement could not be read.

From its /non article, sentences 12, 16, 18

The museum re-creates works written on software since made obsolete by migrating them to newer systems, each listed under its author with the year of its re-creation, Stuart Moulthrop's Victory Garden in 2022 among them; where re-creation is not yet possible, it provides as much metadata as possible.

From its /non article, sentences 33, 34

Under whose name a work is held

Author collections hold an author's own digital works with donated materials. The Marjorie C. Luesebrink Collection holds 66 works, 27 of them created and published under the pen name M.D. Coverley, and the pen name stands at the level of the work: the ELO Works collection names "M. D. Coverley’s Egypt: The Book of Going Forth by Day". Work-level listings name each work by creator, date, format and title, and a restored work, Thomas M. Disch's Amnesia: Restored, stands under its original author. The Decameron Collective, "a virtual world collaboratively designed by of nine Canadian artists and scholars", stands under its collective's name.

From its /non article, sentences 20, 21, 22, 23, 30

Place in the field

The authorship census reads the Coverley name as "a pseudonym kept and traceable to its bearer", with no distinct authorial function constituted under it. The home page lists "Artificial Intelligence (AI)" among the qualities of its collections without naming the works, and neither census finds an attribution rule for machine participation. The Capture Registry reads Google's Overview at the museum's name as an accurate composition of what it is, who made it and who runs it, and records that the composition names no author, where a card names a work under the pen name.
