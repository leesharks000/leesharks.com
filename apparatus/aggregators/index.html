Meta-Aggregators of the Crimson Hexagon

Authorship and the chosen name: a meta-aggregator

A census of the archives, editions, wikis, registries and legal records that hold authorship: avant-garde and experimental archives, works held under heteronyms and pseudonyms, collective and transformative authorship, and the law, policy and certification of AI authorship. Each resource is read from its own pages. Every field is quoted from a saved copy of the resource's own pages, with the date read and a hash of the copy. Compiled 2026-11-01; editor Lee Sharks.

The census asks of each resource: Whose authorship does it keep, under what name, and how does it hold a name the author chose?

The question joins two deposits: EA-HETERONYMY-01 v0.2: Heteronymy as Ethical Operation Against Civil-Name Reduction (AXN:03B8, #940), on the heteronym as an ethical operation against reduction to the civil name, and AI Calls You Fake: The Existence Tax on Real People and Real Work (AXN:0B2A, #1722), on the existence tax laid on real people and real work. A work made under a chosen name meets three facts in each resource below: whose authorship the resource keeps, under what name it holds the work, and who holds the record and on what terms. This census records which resources show each.

Its sibling census, on whether a work is in the training layer and can be read back there, is the Training-Layer Literature meta-aggregator; the family's other censuses are Labor Vector (labor and AI) and Mary Lee Labor (left politics and labor). The rest of the apparatus wing holds the archive's own provenance, rights and captures.

PennSound

ongoing project committed to producing new audio recordings and preserving existing audio archives; Wikipedia (third party) calls it a poetry website and online archive of recordings of poets reading their own work · the Center for Programs in Contemporary Writing, University of Pennsylvania; directors Charles Bernstein, Al Filreis and Chris Mustazza, editor Michael S.
