PennSound

https://writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/

empirical comparatorAvant-garde and experimental archivesAttribution, credit and identity infrastructureactive
PennSound is the poetry audio archive of the University of Pennsylvania, a project of the Center for Programs in Contemporary Writing, created by Al Filreis and Charles Bernstein and launched in 2005. It hosts free, downloadable recordings of poets reading their own work and others', filed under the poet's name and broken down into singles, one poem to a file.

What it is

It is an ongoing project committed to producing new audio recordings and preserving existing audio archives. It is directed by Charles Bernstein, Al Filreis and Chris Mustazza and edited by Michael S. Hennessey, with Danny Snelson and Kenneth Goldsmith as senior editors; the description on its YouTube channel names two directors, Bernstein and Filreis, with Mark P. Lindsay as managing director.

From its /non article, sentences 1, 4, 5

How it files a recording

Recordings are filed on author pages, series pages, anthology, collection and group pages and in a singles archive, and complete readings are kept beside the singles. The PennSound Manifesto, by Bernstein, requires that all files be named and that sound files be retrievable from a library catalog under the poet's name.

From its /non article, sentences 7, 8, 9

Where the reader differs from the author, a recording names both: John Clare's "Vowelless Letter" (1849) performed by Charles Bernstein; Hannah Weiner's Clairvoyant Journal performed by Weiner, Sharon Mattlin and Rochelle Kraut. A co-composer is credited the same way, the music and performance of one piece being by Christopher Reiner "and included with permission".

From its /non article, sentences 12, 13

What it holds and on what terms

Its historical selection opens with Apollinaire's "Le Pont Mirabeau" of 1913. Its size is given in two units, over 1500 full-length and single-poem recordings and well over 50,000 sound files; its own saved site pages state no count. Recordings come from the poets, from institutions and from poets' estates, and documentation is added over time. Its newest collection, announced on 27 March 2026, holds Charles Ruas's radio programming. The terms are stated on its YouTube channel: "All PennSound recordings are made available for noncommercial and educational use only; the featured writers and artists retain all copyrights to their work."

From its /non article, sentences 16, 18, 19, 21, 25

Place in the field

The archive reads PennSound as a witness of a distinct type: a recording keeps the author's name, the performer's voice and a co-composer's credit as separate attributions, so that a reading of one poet's work by another collapses into neither. Its filing rule is the poet's name, and whether a chosen name is kept as given is not shown beyond the names it files under.

From its /non article, sentence 14

Asked for the name on 1 November 2026, Google's AI Overview gave PennSound as "the world's largest free digital archive of poetry audio, featuring over 50,000 recordings spanning from 1913 to the present". The Capture Registry found that it files everything under author pages and has no place for a reading of one poet's work by another, the feature that makes PennSound a row of the census.

Founded2005 (Wikipedia, third party)

What its record can settle

  • whether a recording of a named poet is held, on that poet's author page or a series page
  • who performs a given recording where the performer differs from the author
  • which reading series and events PennSound has recorded or archived
  • on what terms the recordings are offered: noncommercial and educational use only, with copyright retained by the featured writers and artists (its YouTube channel's description)

What it cannot settle

  • the rights terms of an individual recording beyond the general statement (none stated on its saved site pages)
  • complete bibliographic data for every recording (documentation is added over time)
  • how an author who writes under several names is filed (no naming policy in the saved sources)

Kept apart

  • NOT a commercial service: free download (Wikipedia, third party: commercial 'No')
  • NOT a complete bibliography of the recordings: documentation is added over time
  • NOT a rights clearance record: copyright stays with the featured writers and artists, and no rights terms are stated per recording
  • NOT limited to the poet's own voice: some recordings are performances of one author's work by another
