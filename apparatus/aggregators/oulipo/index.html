Authorship and the chosen name: a meta-aggregator / Oulipo

Oulipo

https://www.oulipo.net/

Avant-garde and experimental archivesCollective and distributed authorshipactive
The archive's reading. The site keeps each work under the name of the member who made it inside the group's collective name, and credits one constraint to another group (the Oplepo); Wikipedia's list carries the second name 'Latis' beside Emmanuel Peillet and reports a rule that membership ends only at death. The archive reads this as collective authorship held by enduring personal names; the saved sources state no position on chosen names.
Kindthe group's own site: 'ouvroir de littérature potentielle', with sections for public readings, members (Oulipiens), publications, news (Faits & dits) and constraints; Wikipedia (third party) calls the Oulipo a loose gathering of writers and mathematicians working in constrained writing
Operatorthe Oulipo itself, on its own site (oulipo.net); Wikipedia, a third party, describes the group as founded in 1960 by Raymond Queneau and François Le Lionnais

What its record can settle

  • which member made a given Oulipian constraint and how the constraint is defined
  • what the group published and read in public recently, under whose names
  • how a two-author constraint (Critique constructive) divides the work between its authors

What it cannot settle

  • the full membership and its history (known here only from Wikipedia, a third party)
  • whether any member's published name is a pen name, by the group's own statement
  • the terms on which the texts may be reused

Kept apart

  • NOT a closed historical archive of the 1960s group: the site carries the members' work to October 2026
  • NOT a catalogue of all constrained writing: it holds the members' own work and constraints
  • NOT a statement on pseudonyms: the one second name in the sources ('Latis') comes from Wikipedia's member list
